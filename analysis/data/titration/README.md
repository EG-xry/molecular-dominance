# Digitized published titrations

The immutable mean/error-bar coordinates are in `titration_digitized.csv`. They come from Hosy and Vivaudou (2014), Fig. 3I, and Clatot et al. (2017), Fig. 1c and Supplementary Fig. 3C. Hosy Fig. 4B repeats a transformation of Fig. 3I and is excluded from fitting. Citations, source URLs, checksums and extraction methods are recorded in `provenance.json`.

Clatot's Supplementary Table 1 specifies mutant input fractions 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75 and 0.90, and Supplementary Fig. 3 labels the mixtures with matching percentages. These agree with the plotted positions. The Results text's 1:10, 1:4, 1:1, 4:1 and 10:1 labels are shorthand for the documented percentages. The main analysis therefore uses the source-stated input fractions; converting the ratio labels literally to 1/11, 0.20, 0.50, 0.80 and 10/11 is retained as a labelled sensitivity. The raw `mutant_fraction_input` column and digitized responses are unchanged. The JSON/CSV retain the legacy machine keys `plotted_positions` (source-stated primary design) and `stated_cDNA_ratios` (literal-ratio sensitivity), with explicit labels and roles. The input-to-assembly fraction equality is a modelling assumption. S2 Fig shows the source-design measurements and fit with the literal-ratio fit as a secondary dashed curve; all results remain in the generated tables.

Digitization used custom Python scripts with NumPy/Pillow and Poppler commands, not a named GUI application: `pdfimages -all` extracted Hosy's lossless raster and the script located tick/bar/error-cap stroke centres; `pdftocairo -svg` exported Clatot's vector drawings and the scripts identified marker shapes and aligned error-bar segments after calibrating axis ticks. Original tool versions were not recorded. The scripts and audit overlays are in `provenance/`. Overlay colours are audit annotations on the source figures, credited to the original CC BY authors. The overlays are not fitted biological models.

`n` in the digitized table is the source recording count for every point, including endpoints with no drawn error bar. Hosy's units are inside-out macropatches from Xenopus oocytes. Clatot's Supplementary Table 1 calls n the number of experiments; the Methods describe whole-cell recordings from HEK293 cells. Neither source gives independent batches, cultures or recording days for every condition. `titration_replication.csv` exports every retained point's n, recording unit, and whether a biological SEM was drawn. The numerical GLS analysis uses recordings treated as independent and supplied SEMs as known Gaussian error scales; cell/patch counts do not establish independent batches. The goodness-of-fit degrees of freedom are J − (n_subunits − 1), where J counts interior fractions; candidate contrasts use n_subunits − 1.

`katp_baseline_currents.csv` records all eight nucleotide-free means, SEMs and patch counts from Hosy's Fig. 3 legend. They vary from 1.0 to 5.3 nA without a monotone fraction trend. The authors attribute variation to the oocyte system and report no construct-specific difference within the same day/batch. This supports normalization but does not prove equal baseline current per assembled channel.

`run_titration.py` shifts all interior fractions through −0.05, −0.02, −0.01, 0, 0.01, 0.02 and 0.05, and increases Clatot's mutant endpoint by 0, 1%, 2%, 5% and 10% of its own WT endpoint. Hosy's measured mutant endpoint and its SEM are retained. All 26 candidates remain in each coding/scenario correction family, including series whose one-profile fit fails; all seven fit tests form a separate family. Both Bonferroni and Holm adjustments are reported. The proportional `g_k=k/n` line is compatible with independent subunit contributions and cannot by itself distinguish coassembly. Hosy's `2*` means equal weighting of adjacent and opposite two-WT arrangements, giving the interior profile `(0,0.5,1)`; it differs from the four-to-two multiplicity of adjacent versus opposite arrangements under random placement.

Generated files beside the analysis driver are `titration.json`, `titration_profiles.csv` (14 rows), `titration_candidate_tests.csv` (52 rows), `titration_sensitivity_ranges.csv`, `titration_fit_sensitivity_ranges.csv`, and `titration_replication.csv`. Ranges record contiguous tested shifts/increments preserving the baseline joint fit/candidate decision. Joint rectangle frontiers require that every enclosed grid point preserve that decision; they are discrete grid boundaries, not exact continuous thresholds or experimentally estimated uncertainty bounds. No favorable isolated scenario is selected.

Supplementary Table 1 contains recording counts only, so it cannot validate the digitized means or SEMs. At fraction 0.10, WT+R104W is ND (not done), S460A+R104W has n=15, and S460A+L325R has n=30; the latter two points are not drawn in the digitized panels. The table also lists WT+R104W+difopein, but that series is absent from Fig. 1c and Supplementary Fig. 3C, so no fraction-by-fraction means/SEMs can be extracted and it is excluded. No zero or other response is imputed for these unplotted conditions.

The Hosy Fig. 3I readout is the current in 300 µM MgADP divided by the preceding nucleotide-free current (inside-out macropatches, −50 mV); Fig. 4B replots the same observations and is not pooled. Clatot Fig. 1c and Supplementary Fig. 3C report peak current at −20 mV divided by each series’ WT or S460A endpoint. Hosy raster resolution is 0.0026 in current-ratio units; its error model uses a 0.01 floor to cover inter-rendering scatter. Clatot vector resolution and the numerical floor are 0.0003 in normalized-current units. The larger of the recorded resolution and floor is added in quadrature to each source SEM.
